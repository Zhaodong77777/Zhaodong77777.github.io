Job market paper

Loyalty, Competence, and the Allocation of Office under Threat

Evidence from Qing China

How does the kind of threat a state faces change which offices rulers give to politically connected insiders? Using annual records of provincial officials in Qing China, 1825–1911, I compare changes in the representation of the dynasty’s hereditary service elite following rebellions and foreign wars. Rebellion is followed by a rise in insiders’ representation among governors-general and governors; no comparable rise is detected after foreign war. The evidence is strongest for the provincial executive, while differences in the threat contrast across offices are not established.
